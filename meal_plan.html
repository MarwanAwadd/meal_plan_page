    • 1 tbsp chia seeds ~5 g fibre
    • 1 banana (slightly under-ripe) or berries lower-GI carbs
    • 250 ml skimmed / low-fat milk ~8 g protein

    Drink it with dinner, the last thing before your fast starts. On lifting days that’s right after training, when it helps recovery most. Whatever whey you buy, aim for 20–25 g protein per scoop and low sugar.

    Fasting window · fasted cardio daily · lifting 4 days

    06Daily & weekly timetable

    How the fasting works. Your eating window opens when you break the fast after fasted cardio and closes with dinner at 6 or 8 PM. Dinner time follows your wake time: wake early and you can choose 6 or 8; wake late and dinner is at 8. Either way you fast roughly 14–16 hours and eat in about a 7.5-hour window.

    For insulin sensitivity · small, meaningful tweaks

    08Blood-sugar tweaks

    None of these change what you eat — just how it’s prepped, ordered and timed.

    1. Bake the potato, don’t mash — and eat it cooled. Chilled cooked potato forms resistant starch that blunts the spike. Your batch-prepped potato already does this.
    2. Eat in order: salad & protein first, potato last. Same meal, smaller glucose rise.
    3. Keep lemon (or a splash of vinegar) in the dressing. Acidity lowers the meal’s glucose response.
    4. Shake: under-ripe banana or berries + a dash of cinnamon. Lower-GI carbs and a small but real benefit.
    5. Move daily. Your daily cardio and lifting do the heavy lifting here; a 10-min walk after lunch adds to it.
    If this is diagnosed insulin resistance or pre-diabetes, treat these as an add-on to medical care, not a replacement — and if you’re ever prescribed blood-sugar medication, check with your doctor before continuing to fast.
    I’m not a doctor. Macro figures are estimates and will need tuning as you go. Get medical sign-off before running a strong deficit with fasting at your starting weight. If energy dips badly on lifting days, add carbs at lunch rather than cutting protein.
