The system

02Two dishes a week: one lunch, one light dinner

Each week you batch-cook two dishes: a hearty lunch (5 servings — two lunches a week are eat-out) and a lighter, high-protein dinner (7 servings). Rotate the pairings across the month so you're not eating the same protein two weeks running.

Week 1Lunch — Chicken & potatoDinner — Tuna & egg salad
Week 2Lunch — Lean beef & potatoDinner — Baked tilapia
Week 3Lunch — Salmon & potatoDinner — Chicken & yogurt bowl
Food safety: cooked chicken/beef keep ~3–4 days refrigerated, fish only ~2–3 days. Prep each dish in two batches across the week (or freeze half) rather than one giant cook-up — especially anything with fish.

For insulin sensitivity · small, meaningful tweaks

07Blood-sugar tweaks

None of these change what you eat — just how it's prepped, ordered, and timed. Together they meaningfully flatten your post-meal glucose.

  1. Bake the potato, don't mash — and eat it cooled. Baked beats mashed, and letting cooked potato chill in the fridge forms resistant starch that blunts the spike. Your batch-prepped, chilled potato already does this.
  2. Eat in order: salad & protein first, potato last. Front-loading fibre and protein before starch lowers the glucose rise — costs nothing, same meal.
  3. Keep lemon (or a splash of vinegar) in the dressing. Acidity lowers the meal's glucose response. Already in your salad.
  4. Shake: under-ripe banana or berries + a dash of cinnamon. Lower-GI carbs; cinnamon has a small but real blood-sugar benefit.
  5. Walk 10–15 min after lunch. Not food, but one of the most effective ways to pull glucose out of the blood after your biggest carb meal.
Your fruit choices already help — the low-GI picks eaten with breakfast protein are exactly right. If this is diagnosed insulin resistance or pre-diabetes, treat these as an add-on to medical care, not a replacement — loop in your doctor, especially alongside the berberine/chromium question below.

08Supplements for fat loss / insulin

Straight talk: the diet does ~95% of the work. No over-the-counter pill moves the needle much. Ranked by real evidence:

SupplementWhat it's forEvidence
BerberineInsulin sensitivity & blood sugar (the "natural metformin"). Common GI upset; brand quality varies; interacts with meds.Some
Chromium picolinateMarketed for insulin sensitivity & carb cravings. Usually 200 mcg, low-risk. Real effects are small and inconsistent — fine to try, don't expect much.Weak

Metformin / GLP-1s
(semaglutide, etc.)		The genuinely effective options at your weight and for insulin resistance — but prescription. Worth a doctor visit.Rx
Highest-value move: get basic bloodwork — fasting glucose, HbA1c, thyroid, lipids. That tells you exactly where your insulin sits instead of guessing, and opens the door to the real medications. Clear any supplement with a doctor/pharmacist first, especially berberine.
I'm not a doctor, and I'm not authoritative on what's stocked in Egyptian pharmacies — brands and availability shift. Verify supplement choices and doses with a local pharmacist or physician, and get medical sign-off before starting a strong deficit or self-managing insulin sensitivity at your weight. Macro figures are estimates and may need tuning to how you feel and train; if energy dips on training days, add a carb at lunch or extra egg whites rather than cutting protein.
